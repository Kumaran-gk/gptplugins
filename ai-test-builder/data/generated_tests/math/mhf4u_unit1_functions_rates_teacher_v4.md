## Part B - Multipart questions [50]

**11. Piecewise functions [9]**  
a. Draw `y=x+4` from filled `(-4,0)` towards open `(0,4)`. Draw `y=4-2x` from filled `(0,4)` to filled `(2,0)`. Draw `y=x-4` from open `(2,-2)` to filled `(5,1)`. The first branch's open point is covered by the second branch's filled point. [4]  
b. Domain `[-4,5]`; range `(-2,4]`. [2]  
c. Increasing on `[-4,0]` and `(2,5]` (accept equivalent description of the rising pieces). [1]  
d. No. For example, `T(-3)=T(1.5)=1`. One horizontal line crosses the graph at two inputs, so the inverse gives two outputs for input 1. [2]  
**Thinking move:** Include or exclude an endpoint according to the dot on the graph, and test horizontal lines for an inverse.

**12. Absolute value graph [6]**  
The vertex is `(1,6)` and the V opens downward. `-2|x-1|+6=0` gives `|x-1|=3`, so x-intercepts are `(-2,0)` and `(4,0)`. Domain `(-infinity,infinity)`; range `(-infinity,6]`. The negative sign reflects the parent graph in the x-axis; 2 stretches it vertically by factor 2. [3 graph; 2 domain/range; 1 explanation]  
**Thinking move:** Solve for both absolute-value branches to find both intercepts.

**13. Transforming a function [5]**  
a. Match the original input 16: `-4(x-3)=16`, so `x=-1`. The new output is `2f(16)+1=2(4)+1=9`; point `(-1,9)`. [2]  
b. Require `-4(x-3)>=0`, giving domain `(-infinity,3]`. Since the square root is nonnegative, range `[1,infinity)`. [2]  
c. Reflect horizontally in the y-axis, compress horizontally by factor 1/4, and shift right 3. Equivalently, the point map is `(u,v)->(3-u/4,2v+1)`. [1]  
**Thinking move:** Set the entire new input equal to the old x-coordinate.

**14. Inverse relations [5]**  
a. The original parabola has range `[3,infinity)`, so its inverse relation has domain `[3,infinity)` and range `(-infinity,infinity)`. [2]  
b. The parabola fails the horizontal-line test: `q(-2)=4=q(0)`, so its inverse would give input 4 two outputs. [1]  
c. Restrict the original domain to `x>=-1`. Then `q^(-1)(x)=-1+sqrt(x-3)` for `x>=3`. Also accept restriction `x<=-1` with `q^(-1)(x)=-1-sqrt(x-3)`. [2]  
**Thinking move:** Pick one side of the vertex before choosing the square-root sign.

**15. Circular ride diagram [7]**  
a. Centre height `2+3=5 m`; maximum `5+3=8 m`. [2]  
b. Domain `[0,360]` s; range `[2,8]` m. [2]  
c. Five metres is the centre height. Add the radius again to reach the top, giving 8 m. [1]  
d. Draw one smooth cycle beginning at `(0,2)`, rising to `(180,8)`, then returning to `(360,2)`. Label time in seconds and height in metres. [2]  
**Thinking move:** Bottom + radius = centre; centre + radius = top. Half a turn takes half the rotation time.

**16. Average and instantaneous rates [8]**  
a. `H(0)=3`, `H(2)=-2(2)^2+16(2)+3=27`; average rate `(27-3)/2=12 m/s`. [2]  
b. `H(1.9)=26.18`, `H(2.1)=27.78`; centred estimate `(27.78-26.18)/(2.1-1.9)=8 m/s`. [3]  
c. Complete the square: `H(t)=-2(t-4)^2+35`, so the maximum modelled height is `35 m` at `t=4 s`. [2]  
d. Part a is a secant slope across two seconds; part b estimates the tangent slope near one instant. The ball's upward rate changes with time. [1]  
**Thinking move:** Show both heights in a centred estimate and attach m/s to each rate.

**17. Graph properties and interval notation [5]**  
a. `r(-x)=(-x)^5-2(-x)=-x^5+2x=-(x^5-2x)=-r(x)`. Thus `r` is odd. [2]  
b. Origin symmetry gives `(-2,-28)`. [1]  
c. `|x-3|<=2` means `-2<=x-3<=2`, hence `1<=x<=5`, or `[1,5]`. [2]

**18. Secant and tangent diagram [5]**  
a. Secant `AB` gives the average rate on `[3,5]`: `(5-1)/(5-3)=2` units of y per unit of x. [2]  
b. The horizontal tangent at `A` gives instantaneous rate at `x=3`: slope `0`. [2]  
c. The secant uses two points over the rising interval; the tangent gives the direction at the minimum, where the graph is flat. [1]  
**Thinking move:** A shared point does not make the lines' slopes equal; the second point and interval matter.
