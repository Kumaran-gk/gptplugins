## Part B - Multipart questions [50]

**11. Piecewise functions [9]**  
a. First branch is a downward parabola from filled `(-3,0)` through vertex `(-1,4)` towards open `(0,3)`. Second branch is a line from filled `(0,3)` to filled `(2,7)`. Third branch is a decreasing line from open `(2,5)` to filled `(5,-1)`. The open `(0,3)` of the first branch is covered by the second branch's filled point. [4]  
b. Domain `[-3,5]`; range `[-1,7]`. [2]  
c. Increasing on `[-3,-1]` and `[0,2]`. [1]  
d. No. `F(-1)=4` and `F(1/2)=4`, so an inverse relation would assign input 4 two different outputs. [2]  
**Thinking move:** Check each branch's endpoint, then combine its output values to find the full range.

**12. Absolute value graph [6]**  
The vertex is `(-1,6)` and the V opens downward. `-3|x+1|+6=0` gives `|x+1|=2`, so x-intercepts are `(-3,0)` and `(1,0)`. The y-intercept is `(0,3)`. Domain `(-infinity,infinity)`; range `(-infinity,6]`. The negative sign reflects the parent graph in the x-axis, and 3 stretches it vertically by factor 3. [3 graph; 2 domain/range; 1 explanation]  
**Thinking move:** Check the y-intercept separately by substituting `x=0`; it is not necessarily a root.

**13. Transforming a function [5]**  
a. Match the original input 9: `-2(x-4)=9`, so `x=-1/2`. The new output is `-3f(9)+2=-3(3)+2=-7`; point `(-1/2,-7)`. [2]  
b. Require `-2(x-4)>=0`, giving domain `(-infinity,4]`. Since `f` is nonnegative and multiplied by `-3`, range `(-infinity,2]`. [2]  
c. Reflect horizontally in the y-axis, compress horizontally by factor `1/2`, and shift right 4. The complete point map is `(u,v)->(4-u/2,-3v+2)`. [1]  
**Thinking move:** The inside negative reverses the domain direction; the outside negative reverses the range direction.

**14. Inverse relations [5]**  
a. Original `q` has domain all real numbers and range `(-infinity,7]`. Its inverse relation has domain `(-infinity,7]` and range `(-infinity,infinity)`. [2]  
b. The parabola fails the horizontal-line test: `q(1)=5=q(3)`, so the inverse would send input 5 to two outputs. [1]  
c. One valid restriction is original `x>=2`. From `y=-2(x-2)^2+7`, rearrange to `(x-2)^2=(7-y)/2`. Then `q^(-1)(x)=2+sqrt((7-x)/2)`, with domain `(-infinity,7]`. Also accept original `x<=2` with inverse `2-sqrt((7-x)/2)` and the same inverse domain. [2]  
**Thinking move:** Restrict to one side of the vertex before choosing the square-root sign.

**15. Circular ride diagram [7]**  
a. Centre height `1.5+4.5=6 m`; greatest height `6+4.5=10.5 m`. [2]  
b. Domain `[0,420]` s; range `[1.5,10.5]` m. [2]  
c. Six metres is the centre height, not the top. Add one more radius of 4.5 m to get 10.5 m. [1]  
d. Draw a smooth cycle through `(0,1.5)`, `(105,6)`, `(210,10.5)`, `(315,6)`, and `(420,1.5)`. Label time in seconds and height in metres. [2]  
**Thinking move:** Divide the rotation time by four to locate the quarter-turn heights.

**16. Average and instantaneous rates [8]**  
a. `H(1)=-1+10+4=13` and `H(3)=-9+30+4=25`; average rate `(25-13)/(3-1)=6 m/s`. [2]  
b. `H(2.9)=24.59` and `H(3.1)=25.39`; centred estimate `(25.39-24.59)/(3.1-2.9)=4 m/s`. [3]  
c. Complete the square: `H(t)=-(t-5)^2+29`, so the maximum modelled height is `29 m` at `t=5 s`. [2]  
d. Part a averages the rate from 1 to 3 seconds; part b estimates the rate close to 3 seconds. The upward rate is changing. [1]  
**Thinking move:** An interval's average rate can differ from the rate at its right endpoint.

**17. Graph properties and interval notation [5]**  
a. `r(-x)=(-x)^4-3(-x)^2+2=x^4-3x^2+2=r(x)`, so `r` is even. [2]  
b. Y-axis symmetry gives `(-2,6)`. [1]  
c. `|2x+1|<5` means `-5<2x+1<5`, so `-6<2x<4`, `-3<x<2`, or `(-3,2)`. [2]

**18. Secant and tangent diagram [5]**  
a. Secant `AB` gives the average rate on `[2,4]`: `(2-6)/(4-2)=-2` units of y per unit of x. [2]  
b. The horizontal tangent at `A` gives instantaneous rate at `x=2`: slope `0`. [2]  
c. From `A` to `B` the height of the curve falls overall, giving a negative secant slope. At its maximum `A`, the curve is locally flat, giving tangent slope zero. [1]  
**Thinking move:** A secant describes change across an interval; a tangent describes direction at one x-value.
