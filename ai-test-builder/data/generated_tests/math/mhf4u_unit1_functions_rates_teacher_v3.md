**4. Inverse relations [5]**  
a. Original `q` has domain all reals and range `[2,infinity)`. The inverse relation therefore has domain `[2,infinity)` and range `(-infinity,infinity)`. [2]  
b. The parabola fails the horizontal-line test: for example `q(2)=3=q(4)`, so the inverse would send input 3 to two outputs. [1]  
c. One valid restriction is `x>=3`; swap x and y in `y=(x-3)^2+2`, then take the positive root to obtain `q^(-1)(x)=3+sqrt(x-2)`, domain `x>=2`. Accept the alternative restriction `x<=3` and inverse `3-sqrt(x-2)`. [2]  
**Thinking move:** Restrict to one side of the vertex. That choice determines which square-root branch is valid.

**6. Average and instantaneous rates [8]**  
a. `H(0)=2` and `H(2)=26`; average rate `(26-2)/(2-0)=12 m/s`. [2]  
b. `H(1.9)=25.37` and `H(2.1)=26.57`; centred estimate `(26.57-25.37)/(2.1-1.9)=6 m/s`. [3]  
c. Complete the square: `H(t)=-3(t-3)^2+29`. The maximum is `29 m` at `t=3 s`. [2]  
d. The first rate averages motion over two seconds. The second estimates the rate only near `t=2`; the ball's upward speed changes during the interval. [1]  
**Thinking move:** A secant slope describes an interval; a tangent estimate describes one instant. Include height-per-time units.
