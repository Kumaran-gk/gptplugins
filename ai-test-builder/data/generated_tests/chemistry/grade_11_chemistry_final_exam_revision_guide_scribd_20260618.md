## Exam Pattern From The Public Preview

- Total questions: 33 in the exam paper, with internal choice in some questions.
- Section A: 16 multiple-choice questions, 1 mark each.
- Section B: 5 very short answer questions, 2 marks each.
- Section C: 7 short answer questions, 3 marks each.
- Section D: 2 case-based questions, 4 marks each.
- Section E: 3 long answer questions, 5 marks each.
- Calculators and log tables are not allowed.

## Main Topics To Revise

- Atomic structure: photoelectric effect, quantum theory, electronic configuration, orbital diagrams, ionisation enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, and periodic trends.
- Periodic classification: blocks, periods, groups, shielding effect, effective nuclear charge, and anomalous trends.
- Chemical bonding: ionic character, hybridisation, molecular orbital theory, bond order, magnetic character, dipole moment, hydrogen bonding, and molecular shape.
- Thermodynamics: state functions, enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs energy, phase changes, adiabatic processes, bond energy, and spontaneity.
- Chemical equilibrium: Le Chatelier's principle, Kc, Kp, reverse reactions, and changes in equilibrium conditions.
- Organic chemistry: isomerism, alkenes, aromaticity, ozonolysis, electrophiles, nucleophiles, resonance, hyperconjugation, carbocation stability, Markovnikov addition, peroxide effect, and bond-line formulas.
- Mole concept: empirical formula, molecular formula, Avogadro's number, and combustion calculations.

## Core Formula Sheet

- E = hv
- c = lambda v
- E = hc / lambda
- Energy per mole = energy per photon x Avogadro constant
- Delta G = Delta H - T Delta S
- At equilibrium for a phase change or reaction condition, Delta G = 0
- Kc = product concentration terms / reactant concentration terms
- Kp expression uses partial pressures for gases only
- For reverse reaction, K reverse = 1 / K forward
- Bond enthalpy change = energy to break bonds - energy released forming bonds
- Bond order = (bonding electrons - antibonding electrons) / 2
- Empirical formula gives the simplest whole-number atom ratio.

## Quick Concept Notes

### Atomic Structure And Periodicity

The photoelectric effect supports the particle nature of light because electrons are emitted only when incident radiation has enough frequency, regardless of intensity. Electronic configuration explains periodic placement and many chemical trends. Across a period, ionisation enthalpy generally increases because nuclear attraction increases while shielding does not rise enough to fully offset it.

Electron gain enthalpy becomes more negative when an atom accepts an electron easily. Noble gases have positive electron gain enthalpy because adding an electron would require a new shell or disturb a stable configuration. Fluorine is smaller than chlorine, so added-electron repulsion can make fluorine's electron gain enthalpy less negative than chlorine's.

### Chemical Bonding

Greater electronegativity difference usually means greater ionic character. Hybridisation affects molecular shape and electronegativity: sp carbon has more s-character than sp2 carbon, and sp2 carbon has more s-character than sp3 carbon. Molecular orbital theory predicts whether a molecule can exist through bond order. A bond order of zero means the molecule is not stable.

### Thermodynamics

State functions depend only on the state of a system, not the path taken. Enthalpy, entropy, internal energy, and Gibbs energy are state functions; heat and work are path functions. A process is spontaneous when Delta G is negative. For positive Delta H and positive Delta S, the process becomes spontaneous at high temperature when T Delta S exceeds Delta H.

### Equilibrium

Le Chatelier's principle predicts the direction of shift when concentration, pressure, volume, or temperature changes. A catalyst does not change the value of K or the final equilibrium composition; it only helps the system reach equilibrium faster.

### Organic Chemistry

Position isomers differ in the location of a functional group or multiple bond. Geometric isomerism occurs when restricted rotation creates different spatial arrangements. Carbocations are stabilized by electron-releasing groups, hyperconjugation, and resonance. In normal addition of HBr to propene, the major product follows Markovnikov's rule; in the presence of peroxide, anti-Markovnikov addition can occur.

Choose the best answer.

1. The photoelectric effect provides evidence that light can behave as
   A. only a wave
   B. only a magnetic field

   A. T Delta S is greater than Delta H
   B. T Delta S is less than Delta H
   C. Delta S equals zero

   A. the exact value of Kc
   B. the direction an equilibrium shifts after stress
   C. the molecular mass of a compound

4. Which is not a state function?
   A. enthalpy
   B. internal energy
   C. entropy
   D. work

5. The isomerism shown by propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol is
   A. chain isomerism
   B. position isomerism

6. At dynamic equilibrium,
   A. both forward and reverse reactions stop

21. Electron gain enthalpy is the enthalpy change when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom to form a gaseous anion.

22. Fluorine is very small, so the incoming electron experiences strong electron-electron repulsion in a compact 2p orbital. Chlorine is larger, so the added electron is accommodated with less repulsion.

23. Kc = [SO3]^2 / ([SO2]^2[O2]) = (1.90)^2 / ((0.60)^2(0.82)) = 12.2 approximately.

24. Electrophiles accept electron pairs, such as H+ or BF3. Nucleophiles donate electron pairs, such as OH- or NH3.

25. E per photon = hc/lambda = (6.626 x 10^-34)(3.00 x 10^8)/(242 x 10^-9) = 8.21 x 10^-19 J. Per mole = 8.21 x 10^-19 x 6.022 x 10^23 = 4.94 x 10^5 J/mol = 494 kJ/mol.

26. In NH3, the bond dipoles and lone-pair direction reinforce each other. In NF3, the N-F bond dipoles oppose the lone-pair contribution, reducing the net dipole moment.

27. Ethyne can be converted to benzene by cyclic polymerisation: three molecules of ethyne combine to form benzene under suitable heat and catalyst conditions.

28. Alkyl groups release electron density through the positive inductive effect and hyperconjugation, reducing the electron deficiency at the positively charged carbon.

29. A. f-block. B. Same valence electron arrangement. C. s > p > d > f shielding. D. To keep the periodic table compact while preserving periodic relationships.

30. A. methyl < primary < secondary < tertiary. B. Resonance delocalizes positive charge. C. More alkyl groups donate electron density and allow more hyperconjugation. D. Allylic carbocations are resonance stabilized.

31. Normal HBr addition to propene gives 2-bromopropane by Markovnikov's rule through the more stable secondary carbocation. With peroxide, radical addition gives 1-bromopropane by anti-Markovnikov orientation.

32. N2 has 10 valence electrons. Filling the molecular orbitals gives bond order 3. All electrons are paired, so N2 is diamagnetic.

34. Bonds broken = 435 + 192 = 627 kJ/mol. Bonds formed = 2(368) = 736 kJ/mol. Delta H = 627 - 736 = -109 kJ/mol.

35. The photoelectric effect is emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of sufficient frequency falls on it. Classical wave theory could not explain threshold frequency or the immediate emission of electrons once threshold frequency is met. Quantum theory explains light as photons with energy E = hv.

## Final Revision Strategy

- Memorize definitions, but practise applying them to unfamiliar examples.
- For numerical questions, write the formula first and keep units visible.
- For organic mechanisms, identify the intermediate and explain stability.
- For equilibrium, state the stress, predicted shift, and reason.
- For molecular orbital questions, finish with bond order and magnetic character.
